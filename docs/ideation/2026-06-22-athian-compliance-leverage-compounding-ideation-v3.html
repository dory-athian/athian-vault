Topic Context

Platform shape

AI-native CPG supplier compliance platform. A DSL makes requirements machine-readable — typed with unit, threshold, time period, required data, accepted sources. AI handles evidence collection, pre-review, and report generation; the compliance specialist is reviewer and approver.

Pain points

  • Evidence re-entry across multiple systems — no unified data model
  • Humans re-interpret standards before each audit — time and inconsistency compound per cycle
  • Suppliers manage 15–30 portals; aggregation solutions have failed repeatedly
  • EcoVadis/Sedex: $1.5–5K/supplier/year, human-analyst-mediated, no API surface
  • Batch-mode compliance with no continuous drift detection

Institutional context

  • Athian owns protocol DSLs: Bovaer, AMMP, Rumensin, Carbon Intensity
  • Protocol schema: name, version, code, attachments, baselineComponents, projectComponents; Component has generic schema field — intentionally sparse
  • Feedback Hub pattern validated: Granola → n8n → Claude → Sheets extraction pipeline

External signals

  • EcoVadis: 130K members, 500+ analysts, €409+/supplier, 6–10 week cycles
  • Vanta/Drata: $300M ARR via 375–400 API integrations; requires machine-readable API surface
  • Tendify 2024: 2,000+ distinct procurement portals in Europe
  • NIST OSCAL: months to minutes in IT audit; FedRAMP 20x authorization in 119 days
  • EU CS3D Omnibus I (Mar 2026): compliance-by-proxy is where the volume lives
  • SBOM: adoption followed procurement requirements, not spec quality

Top pick

DSL as Supply Chain Propagation Primitive — the only idea with built-in network effects at the buyer layer. Each CPG customer onboarded propagates compliance demand into their supply chain without Athian selling to suppliers individually. The SBOM analogy is precise: procurement requirements drove adoption, not spec quality. That forcing function is already in motion here via EU CS3D compliance-by-proxy.

I-1.

DSL as Supply Chain Propagation Primitive

Standard encoding Conf 92% Cx Medium

A Tier-1 CPG customer publishes their compliance requirements as a machine-readable DSL profile. Their direct suppliers automatically receive a compliance brief showing exactly what they need to demonstrate — without re-entry, without a separate portal login, without translation by an analyst. As the CPG's requirements change, the brief updates automatically. Procurement requirement becomes the forcing function, not spec quality.

Basis, rationale, downsides, meeting test
Axis
Standard encoding (cross-cutting: also Supplier experience)
Basis
external: SBOM adoption (EO 14028) followed procurement requirements, not spec quality — the exact mechanism proposed here. external: EU CS3D Omnibus I (Mar 2026): companies below the 5,000-employee threshold face contractual assurance demands from in-scope customers; compliance-by-proxy is explicitly where the volume lives. reasoned: The DSL already decouples requirement definition from evidence collection. Propagation is the natural extension of that decoupling: a profile is just a view over a requirement set, and publishing it downstream is a document-delivery problem, not a data-aggregation problem.
Rationale
Network effects accrue at the buyer layer, not the supplier layer. Each CPG customer onboarded propagates compliance demand into their supply chain without Athian needing to sell to suppliers individually. The SBOM precedent is instructive: government mandated a format; the tooling market followed. Here, CPG procurement requirements are the mandate.
Downsides
Requires CPG customers to trust Athian as the authoritative requirement publisher, which is a relationship and legal question, not just a product question. Supplier comprehension of a machine-generated brief needs UX work — the brief must be human-readable, not just machine-readable.
Meeting test Creates network effects at the buyer layer — each CPG customer onboarded automatically creates compliance demand in their supply chain without Athian selling to each supplier individually. This is the go-to-market question.
I-2.

Specialist as Exception Arbiter

Review & reporting Conf 88% Cx Low

AI pre-assesses every supplier continuously. The compliance specialist only reviews exceptions — cases AI flags as uncertain, non-compliant, or at the boundary of a threshold. The specialist's job shifts from running compliance cycles to setting and tuning exception thresholds. This inverts EcoVadis's model where analyst review is the product, not the cost. The structural result: one specialist can cover far more suppliers than the current batch-cycle model allows.

Basis, rationale, downsides, meeting test
Axis
Review & reporting
Basis
external: EcoVadis positions its 500+ analyst-mediated review as the defensibility feature — human judgment is the product. Vanta/Drata's mechanic shows the opposite model works at scale: evidence emits continuously, humans review exceptions only. $300M ARR at Vanta (Apr 2026).
Rationale
The defensibility claim in compliance is "someone reviewed this." EcoVadis buys defensibility with human analysts at €409+ per assessment. The DSL-plus-AI stack buys the same defensibility with a specialist reviewing the exception set — the difference is cost and cycle time, not auditability. The specialist's exception-approval is auditable and traceable in a way that EcoVadis's analyst judgment is not.
Downsides
Requires the AI pre-assessment to be calibrated well enough that the exception rate is manageable — if the exception rate is too high, the specialist bottleneck reappears. Threshold-tuning is a new skill that specialists may not have at first.
Meeting test Directly changes how many suppliers a single specialist can manage — the headcount math for scaling the platform without growing the compliance team linearly.
I-3.

Evidence Shelf-Life Monitoring

Evidence collection Conf 88% Cx Medium

Every piece of evidence in the system carries an expiry derived from the DSL requirement's time-period field. The system continuously monitors evidence freshness — not just at audit time but as a live state. When evidence is about to expire, the platform proactively triggers collection before the supplier falls into non-compliance. Analogous to aircraft continuing airworthiness directives: each component has a certified life, and the aircraft is never "audited" — it is continuously tracked. Compliance status is a live computation, not a periodic snapshot.

Basis, rationale, downsides, meeting test
Axis
Evidence collection
Basis
external: Aircraft continuing airworthiness directives: each component has a certified life tracked continuously — the aircraft is never "reviewed," it is always in a known state relative to its requirements. reasoned: The DSL already includes a time-period field. Shelf-life computation is structurally implied by the data model — evidence expiry is a function of the requirement's time-period plus the evidence submission date. The batch-mode compliance pain point (evidence ages between cycles, drift goes undetected) is directly solved by this architecture.
Rationale
Converts compliance from a periodic project to a continuous state machine. The specialist no longer runs audit cycles — they review exceptions. The platform knows, at any moment, the freshness state of every evidence item across every supplier. This is the most fundamental architectural shift the DSL enables.
Downsides
Suppliers must respond to proactive collection requests more frequently than annual audits. Some suppliers may experience higher touchpoints as a cost. The alert window (30 days before expiry) requires calibration per requirement type — food safety certification cycles differ from emissions data cycles.
Meeting test The DSL's time-period field already contains the shelf-life information. This is an architectural decision about whether to activate it continuously or only at audit time — and the answer changes the product category.
I-4.

DSL-Schema-Aware Certificate Parser

Evidence collection Conf 85% Cx Low

AI extracts fields from PDF certificates — EcoVadis scorecards, SQF, BRC, ISO 22000 — and maps them directly to DSL requirement fields. No supplier re-entry required; the certificate is the evidence submission. Because the parser is schema-aware (it knows exactly which DSL fields each certificate type can satisfy), it validates completeness and flags gaps automatically. The supplier's existing compliance artifacts become the data model, not the starting friction.

Basis, rationale, downsides, meeting test
Axis
Evidence collection
Basis
direct: Certificate-as-currency is the stated informal standard: suppliers share PDF certificates (EcoVadis scorecard, SQF, BRC, ISO 22000); manual re-entry per portal is explicitly how compliance actually happens. reasoned: If the DSL defines which fields are required and which certificate types are accepted sources, the parser's schema can be derived directly from the DSL encoding. Schema-awareness is not a separate build — it's an output of the standard-encoding work.
Rationale
Meets suppliers where they are. The certificate is already the artifact they maintain and circulate. Eliminating re-entry is zero behavioral change for the supplier — they share a PDF they already have. The leverage is high: every new standard encoded in the DSL immediately unlocks certificate parsing for that standard's certificate types.
Downsides
PDF extraction quality varies significantly by certificate format, scan quality, and version changes by issuing bodies. Schema mapping requires maintenance when EcoVadis or SQF updates their certificate format. Not all evidence can come from certificates — some requirements need supplier-submitted data with no certificate equivalent.
Meeting test Eliminates the most common evidence friction without changing supplier behavior at all — the certificate they already circulate becomes the evidence submission.
I-5.

Standard-Version Diff → Portfolio Gap Report

Standard lifecycle Conf 85% Cx Medium

When a standard is updated, the DSL diff engine computes which requirements changed and automatically generates a portfolio gap report: every supplier's current compliance status against the new version, before the new version takes effect. Suppliers who were compliant under v1 but would be non-compliant under v2 are surfaced immediately. The specialist never re-reads the full standard — they review the diff and its impact on their supplier portfolio.

Basis, rationale, downsides, meeting test
Axis
Standard lifecycle
Basis
external: NIST OSCAL's profile→plan model: a plan is a view of a profile; when the profile changes, the plan's compliance deltas are computable. reasoned: If requirements are encoded in the DSL as typed, versioned fields, a diff between v1 and v2 is straightforward computation. The gap report is an application of that diff against the current evidence portfolio. The "humans re-interpret standards before each audit" pain point is specifically about standard changes — this removes the re-interpretation step.
Rationale
Eliminates the re-interpretation-before-each-audit pain point for the most expensive case: when the standard itself changes. The specialist's value shifts from reading and interpreting the new standard to reviewing the platform's computed impact on their portfolio. This compounds with each standard version — the institutional cost of a standard update drops each time.
Downsides
Requires high-quality versioned DSL encoding — if the v1 encoding was imprecise, the diff will produce false positives or miss real changes. Standards bodies don't always publish structured changelogs, so identifying what changed may require AI-assisted standard parsing as a precursor.
Meeting test SQF just released a new version. How long does it currently take the specialist to understand the impact on the supplier portfolio? This idea makes that computation instant.
I-6.

Zero-Standard Onboarding via Gap Detection

Standard encoding Conf 82% Cx Medium

Rather than requiring standards to be pre-encoded before onboarding a CPG customer, the platform starts with the customer's existing compliance documentation — audit reports, questionnaires, supplier contracts — and uses AI to extract requirements into a draft DSL encoding. The specialist reviews and approves the draft. Encoding is bootstrapped from evidence of what the customer already requires, not from manual standard authorship. Time-to-value compresses from weeks to days.

Basis, rationale, downsides, meeting test
Axis
Standard encoding
Basis
direct: Athian's Feedback Hub pattern (Granola → n8n → Claude → Sheets) is a validated extraction pipeline — the mechanic of AI extracting structured fields from unstructured documents is institutionally confirmed. reasoned: The bottleneck for onboarding new standards is encoding labor. If a CPG customer has audit reports, those reports are evidence of what they require — the requirement is recoverable from the evidence. Reversing the direction (evidence → requirement draft) bootstraps the DSL from what the customer already has rather than from a blank standard-authoring exercise.
Rationale
Changes the sales motion. A new CPG customer can be onboarded without a pre-encoding sprint — the platform generates a draft DSL from their existing documentation, and the specialist approves it. This removes the "we need weeks to encode your standards before we can start" blocker that slows enterprise sales. Each successful onboarding also improves the encoding model.
Downsides
AI-extracted DSL drafts may be incomplete or imprecise — the specialist approval step is load-bearing, not optional. Customers with poorly-documented compliance requirements will produce poor drafts. The quality of the extracted encoding affects everything downstream, so the specialist review needs to be rigorous.
Meeting test A new CPG prospect says "we can't start until you've encoded our three standards." This idea changes that answer — onboarding starts immediately from their existing documentation.
I-7.

Portal-Submission Ghost Writer

Supplier experience Conf 75% Cx High

The platform generates pre-filled compliance submissions for other portals on the supplier's behalf, using evidence already in the Athian system. Suppliers manage 15–30 portals; aggregation has failed repeatedly because it requires buyer cooperation. Ghost-writing does not — it only requires Athian's evidence data and knowledge of each portal's submission format. The supplier's Athian profile becomes the single source that feeds all their other portal obligations.

Basis, rationale, downsides, meeting test
Axis
Supplier experience
Basis
direct: Suppliers manage 15–30 portals; aggregation solutions have failed repeatedly (stated pain point). Certificate-as-currency: manual re-entry per portal is how compliance actually happens. reasoned: Aggregation fails because it requires buyer portals to become data repositories they are not designed to be (Tendify 2024: 2,000+ portals, all purpose-built for buyers). Ghost-writing inverts the direction: instead of pulling from portals, push pre-filled submissions to them. This only requires Athian's evidence data and a library of portal submission formats — no buyer cooperation needed.
Rationale
If aggregation keeps failing because the data flow direction is wrong, invert it. The supplier's Athian-held evidence is the source of truth; every other portal is a consumer. The supplier reviews and submits the pre-filled draft — one interaction instead of 15–30 separate data entry sessions. Athian's value to suppliers becomes "we eliminate your portal burden," which is the strongest possible supplier-side adoption argument.
Downsides
High complexity: requires maintaining a library of portal submission formats, which change without notice. Liability exposure: if a ghost-written submission contains errors, the supplier submitted it and bears the consequence, but Athian may face reputational risk. Each new portal format requires reverse-engineering the submission schema, which is ongoing maintenance.
Meeting test If this works, it's the strongest supplier adoption argument possible: Athian eliminates the 15–30 portal burden entirely. Worth discussing whether the maintenance and liability surface is manageable.
