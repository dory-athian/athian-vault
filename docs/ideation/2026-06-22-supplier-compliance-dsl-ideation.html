Topic Context

What the research found

Machine-readable standards (OSCAL + ESG)

OSCAL is the clearest precedent: machine-readable JSON/XML/YAML replacing Word SSPs. FedRAMP RFC-0024 (Jan 2026) now mandates OSCAL for all providers by Sept 2027. Critical counter-signal: 100+ Rev5 authorizations in 2025 with zero OSCAL submissions — mandates without tooling don't move behavior. ESG/food safety has no equivalent standard yet. RegScale donated OSCAL Hub open-source in Dec 2025; compliance-as-code is a phrase but not yet a market category outside IT security.

Continuous compliance mechanics (Vanta / Drata)

Vanta hit $300M ARR in April 2026 (69% YoY, up from $250M end-2025). The architectural pattern: 400+ API integrations pull evidence continuously; hourly tests detect drift; AI agent handles end-to-end GRC workflows. The $65B GRC market is the addressable context. Vanta's 95% acceptance rate on AI-generated questionnaire responses reveals the real moat: a rich knowledge base of prior answers, not the questionnaire UX itself.

Certificate-as-currency problem

EcoVadis distributes scorecards as PDF, secure-link, or platform share — no machine-readable export for buyers. Assent's answer to portal fatigue: one supplier response, shared to multiple buyers via their network. IntegrityNext: AI pre-fills questionnaire responses from prior data, bi-directional APIs connect to SAP Ariba/Coupa. The fundamental issue is identity: the supplier has no persistent, machine-readable credential that any buyer can consume without re-triggering assessment.

CS3D / regulatory proxy compliance

Omnibus I (Dec 2025) raised CSDDD threshold to 5,000 employees / €1.5B turnover; compliance deadline July 2029. SMEs not directly in scope, but face termination risk if in-scope customers can't get contractual assurance. EU model contract clauses due July 2027. Key gap: no defined technical mechanism for how a sub-threshold supplier satisfies assurance demands — the contractual requirement exists but the infrastructure doesn't.

Cross-domain analogies (SBOM, AATP/UNTP)

SBOM solved certificate-as-currency for software supply chains: one SPDX/CycloneDX file, consumed by every buyer. Critical lesson: format fragmentation (SPDX vs CycloneDX) delayed adoption. UNTP (UN Transparency Protocol) extends W3C Verifiable Credentials 2.0 to supply chain; livestock extension is the Australian Agriculture Traceability Protocol (AATP), which defines a Digital Livestock Passport extending UNTP for bovine-specific health and intervention data. UNTP targets 1M passports/day by 2030; W3C launched a Verifiable Supply Chain Community Group in Feb 2026.

Athian's position

Protocol DSLs already in production for livestock interventions (Bovaer, AMMP, Rumensin, Carbon Intensity). This is the structural advantage no competitor in the ESG platform space has: the DSL grammar exists, not as a concept but as running code. The question is whether that grammar can be extended to generate evidence schemas, assessment questionnaires, and verifiable credentials — eliminating the human translation layer that currently sits between protocol and evidence.

01
DSL-as-questionnaire generator
A2 · Standard machine-readability & DSL design
Confidence
88%
Complexity: Medium
Description
Athian's protocol DSLs already encode the precise logical structure of a compliance standard — what conditions must hold, what evidence is required, what thresholds define success. The move: make the DSL the single authoritative source that auto-generates both the evidence schema and the assessment questionnaire. A reviewer writing or updating a DSL rule simultaneously produces the buyer-facing question and the machine-readable evidence contract that answers it. Adding a new standard (say, a new scope 3 reduction protocol) costs near-zero questionnaire design time once the DSL grammar is established.

Directional sketch — the DSL rule is the single source that generates both the machine-readable evidence contract and the human-facing assessment question.

Basis
external: FedRAMP OSCAL and RegScale compliance-as-code show this pattern working for IT security: the OSCAL catalog is the questionnaire generator. Athian's production DSLs for Bovaer/AMMP/Rumensin are the ESG equivalent of an OSCAL catalog — the grammar already exists. RegScale reports 90% faster certifications once the machine-readable source of truth is in place.
Rationale
Human translation from standard to questionnaire is where interpretation drift and cycle time accumulate. Eliminating that translation step doesn't just save time — it removes the class of compliance disputes that arise when two parties read the same standard differently. The DSL becomes a shared contract both Athian and the supplier's systems can reason against.
Downsides
DSL grammar must be expressive enough to encode all relevant protocol conditions, including edge cases that today live in reviewer judgment. DSL maintenance becomes a critical dependency — a grammar bug silently corrupts every generated questionnaire. Requires significant investment in DSL authoring tooling so protocol scientists (not engineers) can write and test rules.
02
Evidence Pull Request model
A1 · Evidence collection & re-entry elimination
Confidence
85%
Complexity: Medium
Description
Today's compliance workflow starts from the buyer side: a questionnaire is issued, the supplier fills it out manually. Invert the model. Athian issues a structured, machine-readable evidence pull request — a document that precisely specifies what data fields are needed, in what schema, with what validation rules — and publishes it to the supplier's preferred endpoint (API, farm management system webhook, or even a structured form pre-mapped to their system's fields). Suppliers with instrumented systems can fulfill it programmatically; others get a pre-filled form where each field knows exactly where in their system the answer lives.
Basis
reasoned: The questionnaire model is push-structured: the buyer describes what it wants in human language, and the supplier translates their system's data into that language manually. A pull request inverts this: the buyer expresses its need in a machine-readable schema, and the supplier's system can match fields algorithmically. This is how API integrations work; the compliance world hasn't adopted it because standards aren't machine-readable yet. Athian's DSL closes that gap.
Rationale
The re-entry problem is not caused by the volume of questions; it's caused by the semantic gap between what the question asks and what the system stores. A machine-readable pull request eliminates that gap for instrumented suppliers, and dramatically narrows it for others by pre-mapping fields. The 15–30 portal problem becomes: one pull request format, fulfilled once, consumed by many buyers.
Downsides
Requires suppliers to have systems with API access or structured export — significant portion of livestock producers won't qualify immediately. The pull request schema must be stable and versioned, or every schema update triggers re-integration work on the supplier side. Adoption requires buyers to also accept pull-request-fulfilled evidence, not just questionnaire responses.
03
Digital Intervention Credential (UNTP extension)
A4 · Certificate interoperability & network effects
Confidence
82%
Complexity: High
Description
Athian issues W3C Verifiable Credentials (v2.0) typed to specific livestock intervention events — a Bovaer dose administered, an AMMP measurement recorded, a Rumensin program completed. Each credential is cryptographically signed, tamper-evident, and independently verifiable by any buyer without contacting Athian. Structurally, this extends the UNTP/AATP framework already being piloted for livestock in Australia, making Athian's credential format interoperable with the emerging global digital product passport ecosystem. A supplier earns a credential once; every CPG buyer of that supplier can consume it directly.

One credential per intervention event, consumed by N buyers — the certificate-as-currency problem solved by cryptographic portability.

Basis
external: Australian Agriculture Traceability Protocol (AATP) already extends UNTP for Digital Livestock Passports covering bovine health and intervention data. W3C published VC 2.0 as a full standard in 2025; the W3C Verifiable Supply Chain Community Group launched Feb 2026 specifically to apply VCs to supply chain compliance. GS1 published a VC data model and validation white paper. UNTP targets 1M passports/day by 2030 with livestock as one of the active domains.
Rationale
Athian's intervention protocols map directly onto AATP's Digital Livestock Passport credential types. Becoming an UNTP extension rather than a proprietary credential issuer means Athian's credentials are interoperable with the emerging global DPP ecosystem — a network Athian does not have to build alone. The cryptographic portability eliminates the PDF-re-entry problem by design: there is no PDF to re-enter.
Downsides
UNTP/AATP is still pre-1.0 (targeting mid-2026); building on a draft standard carries risk of protocol changes. Verifiable credential infrastructure (DID resolution, revocation registries) requires operational investment Athian doesn't currently have. Buyer-side tooling to consume VCs doesn't exist in CPG procurement systems yet — Athian would be ahead of market infrastructure.
04
Event-driven continuous compliance engine
A3 · Continuous vs. batch compliance mechanics
Confidence
80%
Complexity: High
Description
Replace the 6–10 week batch assessment cycle with a DSL-evaluated state machine that updates compliance status in real time as farm events arrive. Each farm management system event (an AMMP reading, a dosing record, a measurement upload) is pushed via webhook or API; the DSL evaluates the event against protocol rules and updates the supplier's compliance state immediately. Assessment is no longer a periodic activity — it is a continuous property of the farm's data stream. Buyers get a live compliance status, not a 6-month-old scorecard.
Basis
external: Vanta's architecture is the direct precedent: 400+ API integrations, hourly control tests, real-time compliance state. Vanta went from $10M to $300M ARR in ~2 years by making continuous compliance the default rather than the premium. The structural difference for Athian: Vanta pulls evidence from SaaS APIs; Athian would receive pushes from farm management systems. The DSL already encodes what a compliant event looks like — the remaining engineering is the event ingestion and state machine layer.
Rationale
The 6–10 week assessment cycle is an artifact of manual review latency, not inherent to the compliance logic itself. Once the DSL formalizes the rules, the cycle time is bounded only by data freshness. The business model shift is significant: from per-assessment fees to continuous monitoring subscriptions — a higher-value, stickier relationship. Buyers who currently wait months for updated supplier status can act on near-real-time signals.
Downsides
Most livestock producers don't have instrumented farm management systems with API-accessible event streams — adoption requires a supply-side technology shift Athian can't control. Partial compliance (a supplier instrumenting some activities but not others) creates ambiguous state that continuous models handle poorly. False-positive compliance signals from noisy sensor data could undermine trust in the system.
05
CS3D Assurance-as-a-Service for sub-threshold suppliers
A5 · CS3D / regulatory proxy compliance
Confidence
75%
Complexity: Medium
Description
CS3D (CSDDD Omnibus, compliance deadline July 2029) creates a structural gap: companies above 5,000 employees / €1.5B turnover must obtain contractual assurance from their suppliers, but those suppliers have no defined technical mechanism for providing it. Athian positions itself as that mechanism — a compliance infrastructure product that livestock suppliers use to generate the contractual assurance artifacts their in-scope CPG customers need. The supplier's Athian record (DSL-evaluated, continuously monitored) becomes the source of truth for the EU model contract clauses due July 2027. Athian is the proxy compliance layer, not another questionnaire.
Basis
external: CS3D Omnibus I (Dec 2025) raises threshold to 5,000 employees / €1.5B — most livestock producers are far below. EU model contract clauses due July 2027 will define what contractual assurance looks like technically, creating a window for Athian to become the implementation layer before that window closes. SMEs "face termination of business relationships if they cannot demonstrate compliance" (Normative/CSDDD research). No platform currently fills this infrastructure gap specifically for livestock/agriculture.
Rationale
The CS3D assurance gap is not a questionnaire problem — it's an infrastructure problem. A sub-threshold supplier can't satisfy a contractual assurance demand by filling out a form; they need a system that generates a legally defensible, auditable record. Athian already builds that system for emissions interventions. The move is to surface the CS3D assurance artifact as a first-class output alongside the emissions record.
Downsides
EU model contract clause language (due July 2027) is not yet defined — Athian would be building toward a moving target. CS3D enforcement doesn't begin until July 2029, making this a long-horizon play. Legal interpretation of what constitutes adequate contractual assurance will vary by jurisdiction and buyer's legal counsel, creating compliance uncertainty around the product's guarantees.
06
Livestock Sustainability Bill of Materials (L-SBoM)
A4 · Certificate interoperability & network effects
Confidence
78%
Complexity: Medium
Description
Draw the SBOM analogy directly. A Livestock Sustainability Bill of Materials is a structured, versioned, machine-readable record of every emissions-reduction intervention applied to a production batch — organized by protocol, evidence type, and outcome — shareable across multiple buyers without re-entry. Like a software SBOM, it is generated once by the producer and consumed by any buyer's compliance system. Unlike a PDF scorecard, it carries a schema version, can be diffed against prior submissions, and can be verified programmatically. Athian generates the L-SBoM as an output of its existing protocol tracking.
Basis
external: Software SBOM adoption (SPDX / CycloneDX) shows that a machine-readable manifest shared once and consumed by many buyers is a viable model — once mandated. The SBOM lesson: format fragmentation delayed adoption; Athian controls its own format and doesn't face competing schemas in the livestock space. GS1 and UNTP are both working on versioned, machine-readable supply chain manifests aligned to this pattern.
Rationale
The EcoVadis-scorecard-as-PDF is the exact problem SBOM was designed to solve in software: a compliance artifact that's generated once but re-entered many times because it's not machine-readable. L-SBoM reframes Athian's output from "a report the buyer reads" to "a structured record the buyer's system consumes." This is a product positioning shift as much as a technical one.
Downsides
"Bill of Materials" framing may not land with CPG procurement teams unfamiliar with software supply chain concepts. Buyers need tooling to consume L-SBoMs — without buyer-side adoption, the format value is unrealized. SBOM adoption was driven by government mandate (US EO 14028); L-SBoM may need a similar push to achieve critical mass. Format proliferation risk if other livestock platforms create competing schemas.
07
Cross-buyer credential network effect
A4 · Certificate interoperability & network effects
Confidence
72%
Complexity: Low
Description
Once a supplier's compliance data exists in Athian's structured format, every CPG buyer of that supplier can consume it without re-triggering a full assessment. The network value compounds with each additional buyer relationship: a supplier assessed once for Buyer A is pre-assessed for Buyers B, C, and D. Athian captures the network value by charging buyers for access to the supplier's existing record, rather than charging only the supplier. This is the Assent model (one survey, shared to multiple clients) applied at the credential layer rather than the questionnaire layer — and it positions the supplier's compliance investment as a reusable asset rather than a one-time cost per buyer.
Basis
external: Assent explicitly runs this model: "a single survey can provide answers to each of their clients." EcoVadis runs a premium version: suppliers pay to share their scorecard with unlimited trading partners. The difference is that both operate at the questionnaire/scorecard layer — Athian would operate at the verified, DSL-evaluated credential layer, where the evidence is more defensible and the re-assessment risk is lower.
Rationale
The 15–30 portal problem is fundamentally a network problem: each buyer operates an independent compliance workflow with no shared infrastructure. This idea addresses the network layer directly, converting Athian from a bilateral compliance tool (one supplier, one buyer) into a multilateral credential network (one supplier, many buyers). The flywheel: more buyers make the supplier's Athian investment more valuable, driving supplier adoption.
Downsides
Network effects take time to materialize — the value proposition is weak when Athian has few buyers on the platform. Buyers may be reluctant to rely on an assessment commissioned by or for a competing buyer. Pricing model complexity: charging suppliers for assessment and buyers for access creates conflicting incentives around what level of rigor benefits whom.

5 Remove assessment cycle for rules-expressible standards Subsumed by idea #4 (event-driven continuous compliance engine), which is the mechanism that achieves this.

7 Athian as a standard-setter (1000x supplier constraint flip) Too abstract — the constraint flip is a useful pressure test but not a discrete product direction. The network effect idea (#7) and UNTP extension idea (#3) capture the actionable outputs.
8 Buyer's compliance knowledge base as Vanta-style AI responder Addresses the wrong end — automates the buyer's questionnaire review, not the supplier's evidence burden. Vanta-adjacent and not differentiated for livestock.
