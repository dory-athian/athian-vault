Axis 01

Standard Encoding

1 idea
Finance

Leveraged lending — bond indenture covenant monitoring

Mechanism transferred Continuous threshold monitoring: typed metric + bound + time window + evidence cadence + reset cycle — evaluated automatically as evidence arrives, not on a schedule.

Compliance Requirements as Monitored Financial Covenants

Bond indentures define forward-looking behavioral constraints — minimum coverage ratios, maximum leverage ratios — that must be satisfied continuously, not just at audit time. Covenant monitoring systems ingest reported financials as they arrive, compute ratios against typed thresholds, and flag headroom in real time. A DSL compliance requirement (e.g., "methane intensity ≤ 0.47 kg CO₂e/kg product, rolling 12-month average, third-party verified, quarterly update") is structurally identical: typed metric, threshold, time window, evidence source, reset cadence. The DSL encodes covenant terms. Continuous re-assessment is the covenant monitoring engine. Evidence drift detection is headroom warning.

reasoned:

Covenant monitoring solves the exact constraint named in the grounding: "batch-mode compliance with no continuous re-assessment." Both systems track whether a measured value crosses a typed bound over a rolling window, with defined evidence sources. The pain of covenant breach (debt acceleration) vs. compliance lapse (supply chain disqualification) creates identical incentives for continuous rather than periodic monitoring. Covenant definitions are also versioned, counterparty-negotiated objects — directly analogous to how standards change and require DSL versioning.

Why it matters

Reframes "did you pass last quarter's audit?" into "how much headroom do you have right now?" That shift changes supplier behavior — proactive evidence collection vs. reactive audit prep — and unlocks evidence drift detection as a native platform capability rather than a bolt-on.

Downsides

Requires suppliers to produce and submit evidence on a continuous cadence, not just at audit time. Behavior change for suppliers who are used to annual document drops. Works best when evidence sources can be queried automatically rather than requiring manual submission.

Meeting test

The covenant monitoring analogy directly imports the vocabulary for headroom alerts, cure periods, and waiver mechanics — does that change how we design the compliance dashboard and supplier notification system?

Axis 02

Evidence Collection

1 idea
International Trade

CITES — Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species permit system

Mechanism transferred Evidence-as-reusable-permit: a third-party-issued document with a defined validity window travels with the specimen across border crossings, accumulating custody endorsements rather than being re-issued at each crossing.

Compliance Evidence as CITES-Style Reusable Permits with Chain-of-Custody

CITES issues permits for individual trade transactions that travel with the specimen — each shipment carries a permit referencing source documentation, with chain-of-custody entries at each border crossing. The permit is not re-issued at every border; it accumulates endorsements. A supplier's ISO 22000 certificate or SQF audit report functions the same way: issued by an authority, carrying a validity window, traveling with the supplier's evidence package across all compliance contexts without re-entry. The Athian portable evidence model is the permit. DSL schema validation is the border inspection. The compliance report is the final endorsement.

external:

The grounding notes "certificate-as-currency: suppliers share PDF certificates (EcoVadis scorecard, SQF, BRC, ISO 22000) as the informal standard." CITES formalizes exactly that intuition with a chain-of-custody model. The structural match: third-party-issued document, defined validity period, accepted use contexts, chain-of-custody requirement when crossing compliance "borders." The pain point being solved — "evidence re-entry across multiple systems (no unified data model)" — is the port-of-entry problem: every border crossing demands re-inspection of the same document.

Why it matters

Encoding evidence as portable, chain-of-custody-tracked permits with formal validity windows makes the DSL's portable evidence model legally defensible rather than merely convenient. It imports a provenance vocabulary — who issued it, when last validated, what its use contexts have been — that the current "PDF certificate" informal standard lacks entirely.

Downsides

Chain-of-custody logging adds data infrastructure. Requires defining what counts as a "custody handoff" in the compliance context — supplier submission, AI ingestion, specialist review each need to be distinct custody events. May over-engineer the MVP if most suppliers have simple evidence sets.

Meeting test

CITES chain-of-custody as the evidence provenance model — does that directly shape how we store, validate, and present third-party certificates across multiple customer relationships?

Axis 03

Supplier Experience

2 ideas
Agriculture

USDA National Organic Program — Certificate of Operation

Mechanism transferred Portable scope-bounded credential: an accredited certifier issues a Certificate of Operation with exact scope boundaries (specific parcels, production units), valid for one year, usable across all market channels that require organic attestation — once issued, no re-attestation per channel.

Supplier DSL Profile as NOP Certificate of Operation — Living, Portable Scope Document

The USDA NOP issues each certified operation a Certificate of Operation defining exactly what is certified — specific parcels, production units, handling operations — that travels to any buyer or market channel requiring organic attestation. The certificate is scope-bounded (a certified corn field does not certify the adjacent non-organic soybean field), third-party-issued, and updated annually. A supplier's DSL profile — their declared attributes, intervention enrollments, verified evidence, and scope boundaries — is structurally a Certificate of Operation: a living, versioned document defining what the platform has verified, at what scope, with what evidence authority, valid through what date. One profile, queryable by any enrolled CPG buyer.

external:

The USDA NOP Certificate of Operation is documented in the USDA Organic Integrity Database (ams.usda.gov/organic-integrity). The structural match: NOP solves the multi-buyer, multi-channel problem (one cert travels to farmers' markets, retailers, distributors, and export markets) using a scope-bounded, third-party-issued, portable document. The grounding describes "portable evidence model across compliance contexts without re-entry" as the DSL's advantage — the NOP certificate model is the field-validated implementation of that design. The 2,000+ distinct procurement portals problem (Tendify 2024) is the buyer-side analog of the multi-channel distribution problem NOP certificates solved.

Why it matters

Encoding the supplier's DSL profile as a formal, scope-bounded, versioned Certificate of Operation creates a portable credential across all enrolled CPG buyer relationships — a supplier verified once presents their profile to any buyer, eliminating per-buyer re-assessment. This is the network effect the platform needs to justify its single-portal model.

Downsides

The NOP model relies on third-party certifiers to issue the certificate; Athian would need to establish itself as the equivalent certification authority — a significant positioning and trust claim. Requires building the multi-buyer query infrastructure before the certificate model has network value.

Meeting test

DSL profile as portable Certificate of Operation across multiple CPG buyer relationships — is that the network effect design decision that should shape both the product architecture and the go-to-market model?

Immigration

USCIS naturalization and visa applications — conditional document checklist model

Mechanism transferred Profile-driven conditional checklist: what documentation is required depends deterministically on prior answers (citizenship by birth or naturalization? prior military? travel history?) — the full evidence requirement is known at intake, not discovered at review.

Supplier Onboarding as Conditional Document Checklist — Complete Evidence Roadmap at Intake

Immigration petition processes (USCIS N-400, DS-260) present applicants with a conditional document checklist — what you need depends on your profile answers, and the checklist is deterministic given the answers. Omitting a required document stalls the whole process; but crucially, the applicant knows the full requirement set at intake, not mid-process. A supplier's compliance requirement set is similarly conditional: a Brazilian beef producer needs different evidence than an Irish dairy operation. The DSL's requirement profiles should generate a complete, conditional evidence checklist at onboarding — the supplier receives a full evidence roadmap based on their declared profile, not a blank portal that reveals requirements incrementally through failed submissions.

reasoned:

The grounding notes "suppliers manage 15-30 portals; aggregation solutions have failed repeatedly" — the failure mode is that suppliers face requirements piecemeal across portals with no unified view. The immigration conditional checklist was designed for exactly this case: a large population of applicants (millions of immigrants, thousands of suppliers) each facing a unique but deterministic combination of requirements, where the institutional cost of handling incomplete submissions is high enough that front-loaded clarity is worth engineering. The DSL's typed requirement definitions with accepted-source constraints are the raw material for generating this checklist.

Why it matters

A profile-generated conditional evidence checklist at onboarding is the single-portal experience the DSL was designed to deliver — and the opposite of the "discover gaps at audit" experience that creates re-entry pain. It converts compliance from something that happens to suppliers into something they can plan and budget for.

Downsides

Requires mapping the conditional requirement logic into the DSL's profile attribute system before it can generate checklists. If supplier profiles are inaccurate at intake, the checklist is wrong — garbage in, garbage out. Initial profile data quality is a real onboarding challenge.

Meeting test

Profile-driven conditional evidence checklist at supplier onboarding — would this be the flagship supplier experience differentiator against the fragmented 15-portal status quo?

Axis 04

Review & Reporting

2 ideas
Maritime

IMO ISM Code — Designated Person Ashore (DPA)

Mechanism transferred Named accountability with override log: the DPA is a specific individual with documented authority, direct access to senior management, and an immutable record of every safety-related decision — creating legal defensibility through documented independent judgment, not just documented outcome.

Compliance Specialist Role as ISM Designated Person Ashore — Immutable Override Log and Accountability Surface

The ISM Code (SOLAS Chapter IX) requires every shipping company to designate a "Designated Person Ashore" — a named individual with direct access to senior management and defined authority to intervene in safety matters, whose decisions are recorded in the Safety Management System. The DPA is not a role label; it is a governance accountability structure with an audit trail. The Athian compliance specialist is structurally a DPA: they review AI pre-assessments, override them, approve reports, sign off on compliance status. But the platform currently lacks the governance layer recording why an override was made, by whom, with what authority. The DSL should include a specialist authority model with an immutable override log.

external:

The ISM Code is available at IMO MSC-MEPC.7/Circ.8 with a well-documented DPA model. The structural match: a human professional reviews machine-generated (or automated) assessments, has override authority, and must demonstrate due diligence to a regulatory or contractual auditor. The grounding explicitly states that EcoVadis positions "analyst review as legal defensibility feature" — the DPA model provides the governance architecture making human review legally defensible, not just operationally present. Without an override log, a specialist's approval is legally indistinguishable from rubber-stamping AI output.

Why it matters

An immutable override log with rationale captures the specialist's judgment in a form that survives personnel turnover, satisfies CS3D/CSDD due diligence requirements, and makes the AI pre-review model defensible in audit — the specialist is demonstrably exercising independent judgment, not approving machine output. This directly answers the EcoVadis legal defensibility argument without requiring 500 analysts.

Downsides

Adds a required documentation step for specialists — every override must include a typed rationale. Specialists who are used to informal review processes may experience this as bureaucratic overhead. Needs thoughtful UX to make override logging feel like annotation, not compliance theater.

Meeting test

DPA-style immutable override log for compliance specialist decisions — is this the governance feature that enterprise buyers subject to CS3D/CSDD would require, making it a differentiator against EcoVadis's analyst model?

Games

Console platform certification — Nintendo TRC / Sony TCR lotcheck

Mechanism transferred Machine-checked acceptance gates before human queue: hundreds of typed technical requirements are evaluated automatically; human reviewers receive only the cases machine checks cannot resolve — a judgment queue, not a universal review queue.

Compliance Specialist Queue as Lotcheck — Machine-Checked Gates Before Human Review

Console platform holders (Nintendo, Sony, Microsoft) require game developers to pass "lotcheck" — a structured submission process with hundreds of defined technical requirements (TRCs/TCRs), most machine-checked in automated passes before any human evaluator engages. The human reviewer only handles judgment calls machine checks cannot resolve. A supplier's evidence package submitted against DSL typed requirements should pass machine-checked gates first — is the document dated within validity window? does the reported value pass the threshold? is the certificate from an approved source? — before any compliance specialist sees it. The specialist queue should contain only residual judgment calls, not uniform review of every submission.

external:

Nintendo's TRC checklist has a public developer-facing equivalent; Sony's certification documentation is available to registered developers. The insight: most compliance checks are binary and machine-executable; human review time is expensive and should be reserved for non-binary cases. The grounding notes EcoVadis's "human-analyst-mediated" model as a pain point — lotcheck is the published pattern that breaks that bottleneck in a high-stakes certification context. The DSL's typed requirements with units, thresholds, time periods, and accepted sources are precisely the machine-checkable definitions TRC entries encode.

Why it matters

If the specialist only sees residual judgment calls, the platform handles 10× the supplier volume with the same specialist headcount — the core scalability argument against EcoVadis's 500-analyst model. The DSL already enables this; the lotcheck analogy makes the UX and workflow design explicit and gives the team a reference implementation to study.

Downsides

Partial overlap with the automated mortgage underwriting triage idea (Clear / Conditional / Refer). Lotcheck is more specific about the pre-queue gate mechanism; the triage model is more about the output tier structure. Both are needed, and they're complementary rather than redundant, but the design team should treat them as one integrated workflow rather than two separate features.

Meeting test

Lotcheck-style machine-checked gates as the compliance specialist queue model — would that directly shape how we design the evidence submission pipeline and the specialist's review interface in the next sprint?

Axis 05

Standard Lifecycle

1 idea
Process Safety

OSHA PSM — Management of Change (MOC) procedure (29 CFR 1910.119)

Mechanism transferred Gated activation: before a process change goes live, a documented impact assessment, sign-off, and employee communication is required — preventing a deliberate system modification from creating inadvertent non-compliance without the organization's knowledge.

Standard Amendments as PSM Management of Change — Impact Assessment Before Re-Assessment Fires

OSHA's Process Safety Management standard requires a formal Management of Change procedure before any modification to a covered process — even temporary changes trigger a documented impact assessment and sign-off before the change goes live. The analogy: when a standard is amended (e.g., RSPO changes its deforestation verification window from 5 years to 3), this is a "process change" that could trigger inadvertent non-compliance for suppliers previously assessed as compliant. The DSL's standard-lifecycle mechanism should gate the new standard version's activation behind a MOC-style impact assessment: which active compliance plans are affected, what evidence gaps are created, which suppliers must be notified — before the re-assessment cycle fires.

external:

OSHA PSM MOC is the canonical solved pattern for: a parameter safe yesterday may become unsafe today because of a deliberate process change, not a failure. The structural match: versioned specifications with compliance implications; transitions between versions must not create phantom failures; impact assessment, documentation, and stakeholder notification are required before activation. The existing "Airworthiness Directive" analogy in the companion doc focuses on propagation (informing affected parties after a change). MOC focuses on gating (you cannot activate the change until impact assessment is complete). These are orthogonal problems in the standard lifecycle.

Why it matters

Without a MOC analog, a standard update activates immediately and silently triggers re-assessment failures for suppliers who were compliant under the prior version — creating noise, customer confusion, and potential legal exposure for the CPG buyer. MOC gating gives the compliance team visibility and control over when and how a version transition takes effect.

Downsides

Adds governance overhead to standard updates — the compliance team must complete an impact assessment before activating a new version, which adds time to the standard onboarding process. May be unnecessary for minor clarification updates where impact is trivially zero. Needs a tiered MOC requirement (major version changes vs. minor amendments).

Meeting test

MOC-style gating before a standard version goes live — does that change how the engineering team designs the standard-lifecycle state machine and what the compliance team's change management process looks like?
