Topic Context

Platform

DSL-first architecture — requirements are machine-readable (typed with unit, threshold, time period, required data, accepted sources). AI handles evidence collection, pre-review, report generation. Specialist is reviewer/approver only.

Update treadmill

EcoVadis changed Silver threshold from 68 → 72 pts (July 2025–Jan 2026). Gold from 77 → 78. A supplier compliant in June 2025 fails in January with zero behavioral change. ~200 supplier profiles need re-evaluation with no automation.

OSCAL analogy

NIST OSCAL 3-layer model: catalog (standard) → profile (applicable subset) → plan (evidence collection plan). Reduces IT audit duration from months to minutes. Direct structural analogue to Athian's DSL architecture.

Emerging machine-readable standards

EU DPP mandates JSON-LD / GS1 Digital Link / EPCIS (first category: batteries, Feb 2027). W3C Verifiable Credentials 2.0 reached Recommendation status May 2025. CSRD iXBRL live for EU filers.

Current platform economics

EcoVadis/Sedex: fixed schema per platform. Adding a new standard requires custom vendor development — $1,500–$5,000+ per supplier per year. 14 weeks/yr average audit prep for orgs managing 3+ frameworks (2025 ISACA).

Build once, comply everywhere

Validated pattern in infosec GRC (ISO 27001 + SOC 2 + NIST CSF share 60–90% of controls). Not yet applied to CPG supply chain sustainability compliance. DORA Ch.V + NIS2 Art.21 + ISO 27001 A.5.19-A.5.21 share supply chain management requirements.

01
Cross-cutting — supplier burden & standard lifecycle

Compliance Inbox Inversion

Conf: High Cx: Medium

Today compliance flows as request → response: the CPG sends a questionnaire, the supplier answers it, the CPG reviews it. Athian inverts this entirely. Suppliers who maintain their compliance data proactively publish their state into the platform — evidence registry updated, certifications tracked, metrics flowing — and CPGs subscribe to suppliers rather than requesting from them. A CPG adds Supplier X to their monitored set and immediately receives current compliance status against the CPG's own DSL standard. No request sent. No response awaited. The 350 annual requests collapse into a subscription update.

Left: today's request-response loop — 350 requests/yr, <50% response rate. Right: Athian's publish-subscribe inversion — supplier state is always current, CPG subscribes rather than requests.

Frame move
Inversion — who initiates the compliance exchange. Today: CPG requests → supplier responds. Inverted: supplier publishes → CPG subscribes. The 350-request annual cycle becomes infrastructure rather than labor.
Basis
ReasonedThe request-response model assumes evidence is point-in-time and must be freshly collected. When evidence is continuous (live ISO cert validity, streaming emissions data), the request becomes redundant — supplier state is always current and subscribable.

DirectAthian DSL has time_period and required_data typed fields — exactly the metadata needed to define a subscription predicate against a supplier's live evidence state.

ExternalFinancial data feed pattern (Bloomberg, Refinitiv) — publishers push; subscribers request once and receive updates continuously. Same structural move applied to compliance state rather than price/quote data.
Why it matters
The 1,050-hour annual burden is a request-response artifact. If Athian is the clearing infrastructure and suppliers publish into it proactively, most requests are pre-answered before they're sent. This inverts the cost structure from supplier labor to platform infrastructure.
Downsides
Requires supplier buy-in to publish proactively — only works when suppliers see value in being discoverable. The network effect is needed: a platform where only 10% of suppliers publish proactively still requires 90% of requests to be sent manually. Chicken-and-egg problem.
Meeting test
"What if suppliers published their compliance state and CPGs subscribed to it — like financial data feeds — instead of sending 350 requests a year and waiting for responses?"
02
Cross-standard overlap and deduplication

Universal Control Map

Conf: High Cx: Medium

Athian automatically computes which requirements across its entire standard library map to the same underlying evidence need. The platform's DSL makes this tractable because requirements are typed — a GHG emissions reduction requirement in the CDP framework and an identical requirement in a CPG's proprietary standard both resolve to the same typed structure. The AI recognizes semantic equivalence and merges them into one evidence collection task. The supplier answers once; both standards are satisfied. "Build once, comply everywhere" applied to CPG supply chain compliance for the first time.

Frame move
Automation — AI reads all standards in the DSL library, computes semantic equivalences between requirement types, and automatically merges duplicate evidence needs before they reach the supplier. The deduplication work is entirely in the platform, not in the supplier's workflow.
Basis
ExternalNIST OSCAL control mapping model "supports mappings among any control-based artifacts represented in catalog or profile form, allowing the same mapping mechanisms to be applied consistently across cybersecurity, privacy, supply chain, and other governance domains." Supply chain is explicitly named.

ExternalISO 27001 + SOC 2 share 60–90% of controls. Teams managing separate programs gather evidence twice. "Build once, comply everywhere" is the validated GRC pattern — not yet applied to CPG sustainability compliance.

ReasonedAthian's DSL types requirements with unit + threshold + time_period + required_data — the structured representation makes semantic equivalence detection tractable for an AI that could not do it on unstructured PDFs. This is the architectural moat.
Why it matters
This is the platform moat. Any competitor building on unstructured PDFs cannot compute cross-standard equivalences — it requires the typed DSL foundation. Athian's architecture makes this tractable and defensible. The supplier experience changes from "I keep answering the same question in different portals" to "I answered this already."
Downsides
Semantic equivalence detection at scale is hard — false positives (merging requirements that aren't truly equivalent) create compliance gaps that are worse than duplication. Requires careful validation by domain experts before the mappings go live. May need a "confidence threshold" below which the merge doesn't auto-fire.
Meeting test
"CDP and the retailer's proprietary standard both ask for the same GHG number — we should be merging those into one evidence task automatically, and the DSL architecture is what makes that possible."
03
Standard update propagation

Standard Diff as First-Class Artifact

Conf: High Cx: Small

When a standard updates, the platform treats the version change not as a trigger for manual review but as a machine-readable diff object — a typed delta between DSL versions. When EcoVadis moves the Silver threshold from 68 to 72, the platform emits a structured StandardDelta that the AI evaluates against each supplier's current profile. No human reviews 200 profiles one by one. The AI triages the delta into three buckets: unaffected, newly at risk, newly compliant. Specialists see a triage summary, not a queue of re-assessment work.

Frame move
Automation — the standard version diff is read, interpreted, and triaged by AI without human in the loop. Replaces 200-supplier manual review with a structured diff evaluation. The human reviews a triage summary, not raw profiles.
Basis
DirectEcoVadis changed Silver threshold from 68 → 72 (July 2025–Jan 2026) and Gold from 77 → 78. A supplier compliant in June 2025 fails in January 2026 with zero behavioral change. This is a live, documented example of the update treadmill causing supplier status disruption at scale.

DirectThe grounding brief names "~200 supplier profiles need to be re-evaluated" when a standard changes — no automation exists today.

ExternalSoftware dependency update pattern (Dependabot, Renovate) — emits a diff of changed dependencies with impact assessment; maintainer reviews a triage summary rather than auditing every dependency manually. Structurally identical to the compliance update treadmill.
Why it matters
The update treadmill isn't just the annual refresh — it's the mid-cycle threshold change nobody announces broadly. A platform that surfaces the diff before suppliers notice, and pre-triages which suppliers are affected, is a fundamentally different product than one that waits for the renewal cycle to surface the gap.
Downsides
Requires monitoring external standard sources for changes — EcoVadis, CDP, CSRD updates aren't published via machine-readable webhook today. The platform needs to either build monitoring infrastructure or rely on manual intake of standard changes. The monitoring layer is a meaningful build.
Meeting test
"EcoVadis changed the Silver threshold in January and we had no way to know which of our 200 suppliers fell out of compliance — this diff-and-triage approach fixes that in hours instead of weeks."
04
Standard ingestion and schema publication

AI-Driven Standard Ingestion

Conf: High Cx: Medium

New compliance standards don't arrive as machine-readable DSLs — they arrive as PDFs, sometimes 200-page regulatory documents. Converting a new standard to a platform-native format is currently expensive, manual, and slow — EcoVadis-style platforms charge $1,500–$5,000 per standard. Athian automates this: an AI pipeline ingests a regulatory PDF or standard document, extracts its requirements structure, infers DSL field types, and generates a draft DSL artifact for specialist review. The specialist reviews the AI's extraction, not a blank template. A CPG with a new supplier code of conduct uploads a PDF on Monday and has a draft DSL ready for review by Tuesday.

Frame move
Automation — AI reads regulatory PDFs and generates DSL artifacts; removes blank-page manual structuring work. Specialist reviews AI output rather than authoring from scratch. Collapses weeks of engineering work to a day of expert review.
Basis
DirectEcoVadis/Sedex: adding a new standard requires custom vendor development — "$1,500–$5,000+ per supplier per year" — the cost the automation directly eliminates from Athian's competitive position.

ReasonedModern LLMs can parse regulatory documents for structured requirements (obligation vs. guidance, quantitative thresholds, evidence types). The DSL's typed target schema (unit, threshold, time_period, required_data, accepted_sources) is bounded and well-specified — this is exactly the document-to-schema extraction task LLMs perform well when the target schema is known.

ExternaleCTD analogy: structured electronic submission replaced PDF stacks in pharma, mandated by FDA. Athian can be the eCTD equivalent for CPG compliance standards — the platform that makes the standard machine-readable, not the PDF repository it's stored in.
Why it matters
The speed at which Athian onboards a new standard drops from weeks to days. This changes the sales motion — a CPG doesn't have to wait for an engineering sprint to get their supplier standard onto the platform. The platform becomes as fast as the CPG's standards team.
Downsides
AI extraction quality on ambiguous regulatory language can be inconsistent — thresholds embedded in prose, requirements with jurisdiction-specific carve-outs, nested conditionals. The specialist review step is non-optional; this is a draft-generation tool, not a fully automated pipeline. Compliance errors from a bad extraction are serious.
Meeting test
"A CPG should be able to hand us their supplier code of conduct PDF on Monday and have a draft DSL ready for review on Tuesday, not six weeks later — and this is tractable because we already have the typed schema the AI is extracting toward."
05
Version control and evidence portability

Standard Version Pinning

Conf: Medium Cx: Medium

A supplier achieves compliance under standard version 2.3. The standard updates to 2.4. Under current practice their compliance status is ambiguous — did they pass 2.3 or 2.4? Athian removes this ambiguity by treating compliance records as version-pinned: a compliance record states explicitly "compliant under Standard X v2.3 as of [date], evaluated against evidence submitted on [date]." When the standard updates to 2.4, the old record remains valid for its version. A new evaluation is required only for the delta requirements. The retroactive failure mode — suppliers losing compliance status through no action of their own — is eliminated.

Frame move
Removal — eliminates retroactive compliance ambiguity. Removes the concept of "compliance status" as a single mutable value; replaces it with version-pinned, immutable records. Standard updates become additive (what does v2.4 require beyond v2.3?) rather than disruptive (did we pass v2.4?).
Basis
ExternalW3C Verifiable Credentials 2.0 (Recommendation May 2025) — VCs are issued at a point in time with cryptographic binding to the issuance context. A VC-based compliance credential is structurally immutable — it doesn't "fail" when a new version is published because it's a claim about a specific point-in-time state. The supply chain community group applying VC 2.0 to supplier attestation is active.

ExternalSoftware package management analogy — package.json pins a version; the installed dependency doesn't break when the upstream publishes a new version. Compliance records pinned to a standard version should behave identically.

ReasonedVersion-pinned records + W3C VC cryptographic binding creates a compliance audit trail that is legally defensible, non-repudiable, and immune to retroactive standard changes. CPG legal teams and their auditors want this — it eliminates the "which version were they certified against?" question in regulatory inquiries.
Why it matters
The EcoVadis threshold change (June 2025 compliant → January 2026 non-compliant) illustrates the problem: a supplier did nothing wrong, but their status changed. Version-pinned records with cryptographic provenance give CPGs defensible compliance history. This is what CPG legal and audit teams actually need.
Downsides
Requires CPGs to understand and communicate which version they're evaluating suppliers against — adds version management complexity to the CPG workflow. Some CPGs may simply want "are you compliant with the current version?" without caring about the history. The VC infrastructure adds implementation complexity.
Meeting test
"When EcoVadis changes their scoring threshold, our suppliers' past compliance records should be immutable — we should be able to cryptographically prove what they certified under, not just say 'they were compliant last year.'"
06
Cross-standard overlap / Standard ingestion

Invert Standard Ownership

Conf: High Cx: Large

EcoVadis and Sedex encode each CPG's standard into their fixed platform schema — adding a new standard requires custom vendor development at $1,500–$5,000 per supplier per year. Athian inverts this entirely: the DSL is the standard. CPGs don't configure Athian to their requirements — they publish their compliance requirements as a DSL document that any supplier on the platform can read, validate against, and respond to without Athian engineering work. The CPG owns their standard artifact. Athian is the runtime and the schema. This shifts the platform from "compliance portal" to "compliance infrastructure."

Frame move
Inversion — flips who owns the compliance standard artifact. EcoVadis model: platform encodes the standard, platform owns it. Athian DSL-first model: CPG publishes their standard as a DSL document; Athian is the validation runtime. The CPG's standard is portable, forkable, versioned — not locked inside a vendor's opaque schema.
Basis
DirectEcoVadis/Sedex: "each encodes its own fixed schema — adding a new standard requires custom vendor development. $1,500–$5,000+ per supplier per year." This is the cost the inversion eliminates — when the CPG owns the schema, there is no vendor development step.

ExternalOpenAPI analogy — compliance standard as schema, supplier evidence as conformant document. Any team can publish an OpenAPI spec without involving the platform vendor; the validator (Athian) just needs to be able to parse conformant documents against published schemas.

ExternalNIST OSCAL catalog model — the catalog (the standard) is a separate, publishable artifact from the platform that reads it. "Charting the Course for NIST OSCAL" describes future integration with agentic AI for autonomous risk reasoning — the catalog-as-published-artifact model is the foundation.
Why it matters
This is the paradigm shift from compliance portal to compliance infrastructure. EcoVadis can charge $1,500–$5,000/supplier because they own the schema encoding. When the CPG owns the schema and Athian is the runtime, the pricing model and the competitive dynamics both change. A public DSL registry creates network effects — the first 100 CPG standards in the registry train the AI for ingestion; the first 1,000 become an industry data asset EcoVadis cannot replicate.
Downsides
Platform-level change — requires DSL tooling for CPGs (authoring, versioning, publishing), schema validation infrastructure, and a registry. High complexity. Also: CPGs may not want their standards to be publicly inspectable — a private/permissioned registry variant is needed for sensitive proprietary requirements. Go-to-market is harder when the platform is infrastructure rather than a feature-complete portal.
Meeting test
"EcoVadis makes it expensive to add a new standard because they have to build it — we should make it so the CPG publishes their own standard as a DSL document and we're the runtime, the same way any team can publish an OpenAPI spec."
