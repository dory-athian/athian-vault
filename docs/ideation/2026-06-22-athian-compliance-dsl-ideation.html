§01

Topic Context

The core bet

A DSL that makes compliance requirements machine-readable, decoupling requirement definition from evidence collection. Architectural inspiration: NIST OSCAL's catalog→profile→plan hierarchy.

Suppliers currently manage 15–30 portals. EcoVadis charges €409+/assessment with 6–10 week cycles. No vendor today offers a portable, machine-readable requirement format.

OSCAL adoption — cautionary finding

100+ FedRAMP Rev5 authorizations processed in 2025 with zero OSCAL submissions. Voluntary adoption failed entirely. The mandate (RFC-0024, Jan 2026) with real enforcement teeth is the first mechanism working.

Lesson: superior standards don't self-adopt. Either enforcement or a platform that makes compliance worse without the standard is required.

Non-API evidence gap (Vanta/Drata)

40–80 manual hours remain per audit after Drata's ~80% automation. Screenshot → control mapping is still human judgment. Vanta's AI Data Import (2025) is the first signal of certificate-as-evidence intelligence.

Food safety / ESG structural divide

Food safety = product-centric (COAs, HACCP, traceability). ESG = site-centric (geolocation, labor docs, emissions). TraceGains Carbon Insights (April 2026) is the most recent bolt-on attempt. No platform has a unified evidence schema.

Key prior art and competitive signals
SMETA multi-buyer sharing (Sedex) TraceGains COA IDP (food-specific) FedRAMP 20x KSIs (325→56 control compression) W3C VC 2.0 (2025 standard) EcoVadis 150K+ suppliers / fixed schema IntegrityNext €9,800+/yr survey-driven FSSC 22000 v6 (2023 gap) VA OSCAL: 426pp PDF → 23K lines JSON Trustwell/FoodLogiQ Q1 2026 COA tools Only 13% of businesses use compliance-as-code
OSCAL adoption lesson carried into this ideation: the conversion cost from existing programs (PDFs, spreadsheets, legacy GRC systems) into machine-readable format is the primary adoption blocker. VA's OSCAL migration proves it — 426 pages → 23,000 lines of JSON, and they still did it only under mandate. Any Athian adoption strategy must reduce this cost to near-zero.

1

Protocol-Execution Evidence, Not Self-Report

Axis 04 · Food/ESG convergence Confidence: High Complexity: Medium

Every current compliance platform — EcoVadis, IntegrityNext, Sedex — is a questionnaire system: suppliers answer questions about what they did. For Athian's domain (Bovaer, Rumensin, AMMP, Carbon Intensity interventions), this is the wrong architecture. Evidence of compliance is the protocol execution records. When a producer records a Bovaer feed delivery event, that record should automatically satisfy the DSL requirement "supplier enrolled in methane-reduction intervention" — no separate questionnaire step. The act of following the protocol generates its own compliance proof.

Basis
external FedRAMP 20x KSIs: "Is your security working right now?" — not "Did you write a policy about it?" Same paradigm shift for Athian: "Is the intervention being executed?" not "Did the supplier fill out a form about it?" Also: TraceX dMRV real-time farm emissions data collection pattern.
Rationale
Athian is structurally different from every questionnaire platform because it manages active intervention programs. No other compliance platform has protocol execution records — only Athian does. This makes the evidence-generation native to what Athian already captures, rather than requiring a separate evidence workflow.
Downsides
Requires the DSL to model both requirements and protocol steps, and to define the mapping between them. Protocols that don't generate digital records (manual interventions, certain legacy feed operations) fall outside this model and need a fallback.
Why it matters
This is the architectural move that makes Athian's data moat unassailable. Questionnaire data can be copied; execution records cannot. A supplier's Bovaer protocol log is evidence no other platform can produce on their behalf.

Meeting test: In the current Athian platform, when a producer records a Bovaer feed event, does that record automatically satisfy any compliance requirement in any buyer's DSL profile? If not — this is the gap to close.

2

Semantic Certificate-to-Requirement Mapping Engine

Axis 02 · Evidence acquisition Confidence: High Complexity: Medium

Current IDP systems (TraceGains, Trustwell) extract structured fields from certificates and store them. What they don't do: map extracted data to compliance requirements. That step — "this ISO 14001 certificate satisfies requirement 4.3.1 in our supplier program" — is still human judgment. Build a semantic mapping engine: when a certificate is ingested, its extracted metadata is compared against every requirement in the buyer's DSL profile using semantic similarity. High-confidence matches are auto-satisfied; ambiguous matches queue for human review. Only genuine uncertainty reaches a person.

Basis
external TraceGains IDP: "automatically reads and interprets incoming COAs, no matter the format. It pulls out relevant values, compares them against your specs." Vanta AI Data Import: reads uploaded files, extracts structured data — earliest certificate-as-evidence intelligence in the GRC category.
Rationale
This is the "certificate as currency" mechanism made operational — the explicit question in the research brief. No competitor applies semantic mapping at the DSL-requirement level. TraceGains does it within one domain (COAs to specs); Athian would do it across all certificate types to all DSL requirements.
Downsides
Accuracy on poorly-formatted PDFs ("handwritten certificates reduce extraction accuracy" per the research) degrades the mapping. Requires a confidence-score UI so humans can efficiently correct the 10–20% ambiguous cases without feeling like they're doing full manual work.
Why it matters
Every existing platform either requires human mapping (Vanta screenshot upload) or is domain-locked (TraceGains COA-only). A cross-domain semantic mapper turns any certificate issued by any accredited body into potential compliance currency.

Meeting test: What is the precision/recall of a semantic match between "ISO 14001:2015 certificate, issued Bureau Veritas, valid 2024–2027" and DSL requirement "Supplier must maintain ISO 14001-compliant EMS, verified by accredited third party"?

3

Supplier-Owned Compliance Ledger, Buyer-Subscribes

Axis 03 · Portability Confidence: High Complexity: Low

The current model: supplier logs into 15–30 buyer portals and answers each buyer's unique questionnaire — same data, different forms. The inversion: the supplier maintains one structured compliance record (their Athian ledger), and buyers subscribe to receive evidence satisfying their specific DSL requirements. Evidence is added once; the platform maps it to each buyer's requirement profile automatically. Buyers get live compliance dashboards without managing a supplier questionnaire program. This is Sedex SMETA — but generalized from "one audit report" to "all compliance evidence."

Basis
external Sedex SMETA mechanics: "supplier decides which buyers are granted access to the report on the Sedex platform." 60,000+ SMETA audits/year, CGF SSCI recognition Nov 2025. The multi-buyer sharing model has market validation at scale — Athian would generalize it beyond audit reports to all evidence types.
Rationale
This is the architectural inversion that makes portal fatigue solvable at its root. Not an incremental improvement — it changes who holds the compliance record. The Sedex precedent proves buyers will accept this model when the platform is credible.
Downsides
Requires buyer trust in Athian's verification layer — buyers are giving up direct control over the questionnaire experience. The first few buyer adopters may resist the model. Likely requires a dual-mode period where buyers can still run traditional assessments.
Why it matters
The ledger model gives Athian a structural network effect: each supplier who onboards brings all their buyers with them. Each buyer who subscribes gets access to every supplier already on the platform. This is fundamentally different from the current "one buyer, one questionnaire program" model.

Meeting test: Is this Athian's current architecture, or does Athian still have a buyer-initiates-request model? If the latter, this is the architectural decision that changes the business model.

4

Package Manager Model for Compliance Requirements

Axis 01 · DSL architecture Confidence: High Complexity: Medium

Software package managers solved dependency resolution: a project declares its dependencies, the manager resolves the full tree, fetches exact versions, and reports conflicts. Apply this to compliance requirements. A buyer's compliance "package.json" declares which standards they require (fssc-22000@^6.0, ghg-protocol-scope3@2024, athian-bovaer@1.2); the resolver fetches machine-readable catalogs, computes which evidence types are required, identifies conflicts between standards, and surfaces them before assessment. DSL versioning becomes semver — breaking changes are flagged automatically when a standard is updated. This makes buyer-composed compliance profiles (the OSCAL profile layer) implementation-concrete.

Basis
reasoned OSCAL's catalog → profile → plan hierarchy is structurally identical to package → project → lockfile. The npm model achieved voluntary adoption through network effects (central registry, tooling, discoverability) — the lesson OSCAL missed by not building the registry first. Semver's dependency resolution is exactly what standard versioning (FSSC 22000 v5 → v6) currently lacks.
Rationale
Package managers succeeded by making dependency resolution invisible. A compliance package manager makes standard composition invisible. The central catalog registry becomes the distribution bottleneck that creates platform value without requiring lock-in — analogous to how npm made Node.js ecosystem effects compounding.
Downsides
Requires seeding the catalog with machine-readable versions of the relevant standards — which is the same conversion cost OSCAL encountered. The package manager is only as good as the package registry. Conflict resolution between incompatible standards (two standards with mutually exclusive practices) is a hard technical problem.
Why it matters
Positions Athian as infrastructure rather than product — the compliance resolution layer that other tools compose on top of. This is a much larger opportunity than a compliance portal, and it makes the DSL a network-effect asset rather than a proprietary format.

Meeting test: Can the Athian DSL today express version constraints and dependency relationships between standards, or are requirements loaded as flat catalogs?

5

Compliance Passport as Commercial Asset

Axis 03 · Portability Confidence: Medium Complexity: Low

The assumption: compliance is a cost center and a prerequisite. The reframe: a supplier with a verified, continuously fresh compliance record on Athian has a commercially valuable asset. Build a Compliance Passport — a verified, shareable credential (W3C Verifiable Credential, published as a standard in 2025) that a supplier can present to any new buyer as proof of compliance without re-assessment. Suppliers pay for this because it reduces their total audit burden across all buyers. The business model implication: not just "platform for buyers" but a two-sided market where suppliers also pay for passport maintenance. Combined with a continuous Supplier Compliance Score (updated as evidence freshens or decays), the passport becomes financeable — high-score suppliers could unlock better payment terms or reduced audit frequency.

Basis
external W3C VC 2.0 published as standard 2025; W3C Supply Chain VC Community Group launched Feb 2026. Sedex SMETA: supplier controls which buyers get access — the portability mechanic is validated at 60,000+ audits/year. UN Transparency Protocol adopted VCs for greenwashing prevention.
Rationale
When compliance becomes a commercial asset, suppliers have incentive to maintain it continuously rather than satisfying the minimum before an audit window. This changes the supplier relationship from reluctant compliance actor to invested platform participant — the flywheel that makes continuous monitoring viable.
Downsides
Medium confidence because buyer acceptance of a pre-verified passport (rather than running their own assessment) requires trust in Athian's verification quality. The first wave of buyers may be reluctant to give up assessment control. Also: W3C VC infrastructure is still nascent in food/ag specifically.
Why it matters
Opens a second revenue stream (supplier-pays) and creates platform liquidity — every supplier who buys a passport brings their buyers onto the platform to verify it. The supply chain finance angle (credit score model) is genuinely novel in this space; no current ESG platform has approached banks or insurers as buyers of compliance scores.

Meeting test: Would Athian's current CPG customers accept an Athian-verified Compliance Passport from a supplier in lieu of running their own EcoVadis assessment?

6

Outcome-DSL Layer

Axis 01 · DSL architecture Confidence: Medium Complexity: Low

The implicit assumption in standard-based compliance: requirements are defined by what a supplier does (processes, certifications, audits). The constraint-flip: what if requirements were defined by what a supplier achieves? An outcome-DSL layer sits above the standard-requirements layer. Buyers can define requirements as outcomes — measurable, time-bound — and the platform maps available evidence to those outcomes. "Reduce supplier methane intensity by 25% over 3 years, verified quarterly." This is FedRAMP 20x's KSI model applied to supply chain: 325+ NIST prescriptive controls compressed to 56 measurable security indicators. For Athian's domain, this is particularly natural — emissions reduction is inherently outcome-defined.

Basis
external FedRAMP 20x KSIs: "instead of describing control implementation, providers must demonstrate that security measures actively function." 325 NIST controls → 56 KSIs. GHG Protocol Scope 3: investors require "scientific basis for disclosure, including estimation methods" — outcome, not process. CSDDD compliance deadline July 2029 — outcome-defined regulatory targets.
Rationale
An outcome-DSL layer future-proofs requirements against standard version churn — when FSSC 22000 v7 is released, outcome-defined requirements don't break. It also makes the DSL compatible with regulatory mandates (CSDDD, SEC climate disclosure) that are outcome-defined rather than standard-defined, which is the direction regulation is heading.
Downsides
Outcome definition is harder to audit than process certification — "methane reduced by 25%" requires agreed measurement methodology, baseline data, and verification approach. This is the Scope 3 accounting problem Athian already faces. Medium confidence because the hard part is measurement agreement, not the DSL layer itself.
Why it matters
Most competitors will be chasing standard versions forever. Athian with an outcome-DSL layer is positioned for the regulatory environment being built now — where companies are held to emissions outcomes, not process checklists.

Meeting test: Can Athian express "reduce supplier methane intensity by 25% over 3 years, verified quarterly" as a compliance requirement in the current DSL, or only as a project milestone?

7

LLM-Powered DSL Compiler for Standard PDFs

Axis 05 · Adoption mechanics Confidence: High Complexity: Low

Every compliance standard is published as a PDF or Word document. Current industry process: a domain expert reads the PDF, manually translates requirements into a questionnaire, loads it into a platform — EcoVadis calls this "vendor development" and it's why adding a new standard takes quarters. Build an LLM-powered DSL compiler that ingests a standard PDF (FSSC 22000, SQF, ISO 14064, Bovaer protocol) and produces a draft machine-readable DSL profile, with domain expert review for critical requirements. The VA OSCAL migration cost (426 pages → 23,000 lines of JSON) is exactly this problem — Athian can make migration near-free where OSCAL left it expensive. This is also what makes the package manager model (Idea 4) viable: the catalog needs machine-readable packages, and this is how you produce them.

Basis
external VA OSCAL migration: 426-page PDF → 23,000 lines of JSON; still only done under mandate because of conversion cost. EcoVadis: "fixed schema per standard — adding a new standard requires vendor development." FedRAMP 20x compressed 325 NIST controls to 56 KSIs — expert judgment seeded by structured analysis, not pure hand-authoring.
Rationale
The DSL compiler cuts Athian's standard-onboarding bottleneck from quarters to days. For a CPG customer who needs a bespoke protocol (Carbon Intensity, AMMP) mapped to machine-readable requirements, this turns a 2-month scoping project into a 2-day validation exercise. It's also the key to the adoption mechanics problem: LLM migration of existing EcoVadis / SafetyChain questionnaires into Athian DSL format lowers switching cost dramatically.
Downsides
LLM extraction of compliance requirements from legal/standards language is accuracy-sensitive — a wrong requirement definition has audit consequences. Requires domain expert validation in the loop. Also: standard body IP/licensing concerns on ingesting PDFs and converting to structured formats.
Why it matters
Without this, the Athian DSL catalog will always lag competitors who have more resources for manual standard encoding. With it, Athian can add any standard in days — including the bespoke Athian protocols (Bovaer, Rumensin, AMMP) that no other platform has ever modeled.

Meeting test: Has Athian tried feeding an FSSC 22000 PDF into an LLM and asking it to extract structured requirements? What does the output quality look like without domain post-processing?
